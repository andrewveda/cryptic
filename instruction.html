Anatomy of a Cryptic Clue

📖
Definition
PART 1 or 3
Always found at the very beginning or the very end of the clue. Never buried in the middle. It's a synonym for the answer.
🔑
Wordplay Indicator
SIGNALS TYPE
A special word or phrase that tells you how to manipulate the fodder — anagram, reversal, hidden word, and more.
🧩
Fodder
RAW MATERIAL
The letters, words, or phrases that get transformed by the wordplay indicator to build the answer.
💡 Golden Rule — If the wordplay indicator and the fodder occupy one end of the clue, the definition must occupy the other end. The letter count appears in parentheses at the close.

The Six Clue Types

🔀
Anagram
Rearrange the letters into a new word

An anagram indicator — usually a word implying physical movement, disorder, or transformation — signals that the fodder letters must be scrambled to reveal the answer. Words suggesting physical movement are almost always anagram indicators.

Acrostic
First (or last) letters spell the answer

Acrostic indicators tell you to harvest the initial — sometimes final — letter of each significant word. The number of words in the relevant phrase matches the number of letters in the answer.

Leaders Praise A Rabbit In Scottish City of Love (5)
IndicatorLeaders = take the first letter of each word
FodderPraise A Rabbit In Scottish … = PARIS
DefinitionCity of Love — "Scottish city" is a red herring!
Answer PARIS (5)
🔗
Charade
Word parts assembled in sequence

Charade clues piece together smaller components — abbreviations, Roman numerals, chemical symbols, homophones, truncated words — placing them side by side to build the answer. Implied juxtaposition (no explicit indicator) or words like with, and, joins are common signals.

Strict gold hear drivers turn (7)
DefinitionStrict
Indicatorhear = homophone signal for "steer" → sounds like
AssemblyGold = AU + sounds-like(drivers turn) = STEER → AU + STERE = AUSTERE
Answer AUSTERE (7)
Feline with aluminium — heartless yeast causes a reaction (8)
Indicatorwith = join the parts
Assemblycat + Al (aluminium) + yst (heartless yeast) = CATALYST
Definitioncauses a reaction
Answer CATALYST (8)
🫥
Hidden Word
The answer lurks inside the clue itself

The answer is physically concealed within consecutive letters of the clue — spanning word boundaries, ignoring spaces. Scan the clue character by character until it reveals itself.

Indicatorhides in = hidden word

✂️
Deletion
Remove letters to reveal the answer

Deletion clues signal that letters must be removed — from the head, tail, or heart of a word — before the answer appears. Specific positions have specific keywords.

HeadlessLeglessHeartless AlmostNearlyWithout end LackingMissingCut
Persevering resin without end lies legless with headless ant (9)
DefinitionPersevering — at the start, so it's the definition
Assemblyresin (without end) + lie (lies legless) + ant (headless) = RESILIENT
Answer RESILIENT (9)
⚖️
Double Definition
Two clues, one answer — no wordplay indicator at all

No indicator, no fodder. The clue consists of exactly two separate definitions both pointing to the same word. Recognise double definitions by a very short clue — often just two or three words. The absence of an indicator is the indicator.

Fight or move a boat (3)
Def 1Fight → row, box, spar…
Def 2Move a boat → row, paddle, oar…
OverlapThe 3-letter word satisfying both = ROW
Answer ROW (3)
Costly honey (4)
Def 1Costly → expensive, steep, dear
Def 2Honey (term of endearment) → darling, dear
Answer DEAR (4)

04

Solving Strategy

TIP 01
Locate the definition first — it's always at the very start or the very end. Never in the middle.
TIP 02
Count the letters shown in parentheses. Match them to the length of your candidate answers before committing.
TIP 03
Ignore all punctuation. Commas, dashes, apostrophes — they exist only to mislead you into a false reading.
TIP 04
Spot the wordplay indicator first; once you know the type of clue, the structure becomes transparent.
TIP 05
Be fluid. If one reading of a word fails, try the next. A single word can serve as a noun, verb, adjective, or abbreviation.
TIP 06
Red herrings are planted deliberately. An evocative word or phrase that seems thematic is often designed to mislead your surface reading.
TIP 07
For double definitions, look for very short clues with no obvious indicator — two words or a two-part phrase.
TIP 08
Every correct answer satisfies both the wordplay and the definition. If only one path leads there, keep looking.
TIP 09
Adaptability is the hallmark of genius. Shift perspectives until the solution emerges — the same mental agility that fuels discovery.
